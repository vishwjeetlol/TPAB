From "Introduction: The Needle in the Groove"

"There's a sound that opens To Pimp a Butterfly, and it isn't music. It's the hiss of a needle finding a groove. A tiny mechanical cough before anything begins. Kendrick Lamar could have started this album with a beat, a bar, a scream — instead he started it with the sound of someone about to play a record for you..."

That choice tells you everything about how to listen to what follows. This isn't a playlist. It's not sixteen tracks you can shuffle on a run. It's a story, told in order, meant to be sat with front to back, the way people used to sit with albums before skip buttons existed.

Out of that hiss comes a voice, warm and worn, lifted from a 1973 song by the Jamaican singer Boris Gardiner. He's repeating one word, star, over and over, like a blessing someone forgot they were allowed to give themselves. Gardiner recorded that song at a time when he was trying to take a slur aimed at his own people and turn it inside out, wear it as armour instead of a wound. Kendrick will do the exact same thing with a different word, on a different song, near the very end of this album...
